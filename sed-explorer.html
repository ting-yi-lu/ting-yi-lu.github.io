What does each telescope see of a galaxy at z = 7?

An interactive version of the classic “one SED, many facilities” figure (as in Fig. 2 of Smit & Bowler 2026, ARA&A). Pick a redshift, UV magnitude and galaxy type; the observed spectrum, emission lines, atmospheric windows and approximate instrument depths update together. Drag across the plot to zoom, double-click to reset.

Top axis

How to read it. The curve is the flux a telescope above the atmosphere would see, in µJy (right axis in AB mag). Each short horizontal bar with an arrow is the continuum depth of one filter or band at the chosen S/N and time; a galaxy is detected where the curve sits above the bar. Lanes below show where each instrument works, with the atmosphere on top. Ground-based instruments only see through its windows. Hover anything for numbers.

Emission-line reference

The lines in the model, grouped by rest wavelength. Ticking a row shows or hides it in the plot. The last three columns follow the current galaxy: where the line lands, the model flux, and the selected facility that reaches the chosen S/N fastest (background-limited √t scaling, no overheads).

Model. Stellar continuum: broken power law normalised to MUV (β in the UV, a Balmer/4000 Å step, an optical slope, a Rayleigh–Jeans tail). Star formation: SFRUV from Lν(1500 Å) and SFRIR from LIR (Kennicutt & Evans 2012), split by the obscured fraction. Dust: optically thin modified blackbody (βd≈1.8) with a mid-IR power law and PAH bands (Casey 2012-like), normalised to LIR(8–1000 µm). Radio: FIR–radio correlation (q=2.64) with 10% free–free. Hydrogen lines: case B from SFRtot×ξion boost; metal lines: ratios interpolated in metallicity between three anchor sets; Calzetti attenuation. [C II] from De Looze+14 scaled per type; other far-IR lines as ratios to [C II]/[O III]; CO from the LIR–L′CO relation with a type-dependent SLED and a metallicity penalty (CO-dark gas). IGM: Madau (1995) Lyman-series forest plus the damping-wing toy model. Cosmology: flat ΛCDM, H0=67.7, Ωm=0.31.

Depths. 5σ point-source values for 10 ks on source, rounded from public sensitivity tables and calculators (JWST JDox, HST/ACS–WFC3 handbooks, ALMA & NOEMA sensitivity calculators, VLA exposure calculator, instrument pages for Keck, Subaru, VLT and the ELT design studies), scaled as √t. Herschel/SPIRE and Euclid Wide are fixed survey or confusion limits. They are good to roughly a factor of two and ignore sky lines, overheads, source size and calibration.
