How likely is a galaxy to sit in a large ionized bubble?

During reionization, Lyα photons from a galaxy are far more likely to reach us when the galaxy lies inside a large ionized bubble. Pick a reionization model, redshift and IGM neutral fraction, then a galaxy, an overdensity or a random position, and see how often the bubble around it is larger than the size you choose. The numbers come from the (1.6 Gpc)3 simulations of Lu et al. (2024).

How the numbers are computed

The bubble size around a position is measured with the mean-free-path method: from the position, the distance to the first neutral cell is drawn along a random direction. Positions in neutral gas have a bubble size of zero. Each probability is the fraction of simulated galaxies, overdensities or random positions whose bubble size is at least the chosen radius. Bubble sizes in the simulation come on a grid of radii spaced by 10 per cent, so the probability changes in small steps as the radius is varied.

The two models bracket the sources of reionization. In the gradual model, numerous faint galaxies drive reionization and many small bubbles form around them. In the rapid model, only rarer, brighter galaxies emit ionizing photons, so bubbles are larger at fixed xHI. Overdensities count galaxies with MUV ≤ −19 (mAB ≈ 28 at z = 8) in a 4.84 arcmin2 field with Δz = 0.2, about 2000 cMpc3, relative to the mean number in the simulation box.

Bubble size lists: public data on ERDA, with an example notebook on GitHub. If you use these numbers, please cite Lu et al. 2024, MNRAS, 528, 4872.
